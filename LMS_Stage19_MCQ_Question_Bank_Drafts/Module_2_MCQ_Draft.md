### Question 1 (Order: 1)
**Topic / Category:** Employment Classifications: FTE vs. Contract  
**Difficulty:** Easy  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 1 (Full-Time Employment (FTE) vs Contract-to-Hire (C2H)) & File `3. Hiring Types.docx`  

**Question Statement:**  
Under a standard Full-Time Employment (FTE) model in India, which organization maintains the permanent employment contract and processes the employee's monthly statutory benefits?

- **A.** The candidate's college or educational institution.
- **B.** The client organization hiring the employee onto its direct payroll.
- **C.** The third-party job portal where the candidate's resume was originally hosted.
- **D.** The state government's labor department directly.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why B is correct:** In direct Full-Time Employment (FTE), the employee is hired onto the direct corporate payroll of the client company, receiving company benefits, statutory provident fund (PF), health insurance, gratuity, and performance bonuses directly from that employer.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* because academic institutions do not maintain corporate payroll contracts.  
  - *C is incorrect* because job boards (Naukri, LinkedIn, Indeed) are candidate discovery platforms, not employers of record.  
  - *D is incorrect* because state labor departments enforce compliance regulations but do not act as private employers.

### Question 2 (Order: 2)
**Topic / Category:** Contract-to-Hire (C2H) Operational Lifecycle  
**Difficulty:** Medium  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 1 (FTE vs C2H) & File `3.1. Full-Time Hiring vs. Contract Hiring_ A Comprehensive Guide.docx`  

**Question Statement:**  
In a Contract-to-Hire (C2H) arrangement in the IT staffing industry, which statement accurately reflects the candidate's employment status?

- **A.** The candidate is permanently bound to the staffing agency with no possibility of joining the client full-time.
- **B.** The candidate starts on the staffing agency's payroll deployed at the client site for a defined contract period, with the contractual provision to convert to direct client payroll based on project performance and budget.
- **C.** The candidate works as an unpaid intern for six months before receiving any commercial compensation.
- **D.** The candidate is considered a freelance independent contractor exempt from Indian labor laws.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why B is correct:** C2H enables client organizations to evaluate a technical specialist's delivery quality, cultural adaptation, and problem-solving skills on agency payroll (typically 3 to 12 months) before executing a formal conversion ("buyout" or direct roll-over) to full-time client payroll.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* because the primary purpose of C2H is to enable conversion to client direct payroll.  
  - *C is incorrect* because professional C2H consultants receive regular, full commercial salaries from day one.  
  - *D is incorrect* because agency contract employees in India are governed by statutory frameworks (EPF, ESI, Payment of Wages Act) managed by the staffing agency.

### Question 3 (Order: 3)
**Topic / Category:** Staffing Agency Commercial Fee Structures (FTE Direct Placement)  
**Difficulty:** Medium  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 3 (Commercial Fee Structures: Direct Placement Fees vs Agency Markups) & File `Key Aspects in Fulltime Hiring - PDF.docx`  

**Question Statement:**  
An IT staffing agency places a Senior Java Backend Lead at a global capability center (GCC) on a direct FTE model. The agreed contractual placement fee is 8.33% of the candidate's Annual Fixed CTC. If the candidate joins with an Annual Fixed CTC of ₹18,00,000, what is the professional service fee invoiced by the agency?

- **A.** ₹83,300
- **B.** ₹1,49,940
- **C.** ₹18,00,000
- **D.** ₹3,60,000

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why B is correct:** Standard contingency FTE placement fees are calculated as a percentage of the annual fixed CTC:  
  `Fee = ₹18,00,000 × 8.33% (or 1/12th of annual CTC, equivalent to 1 month's salary) = ₹1,49,940` (plus applicable GST).
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* due to a calculation error omitting one digit (`₹18,00,000 × 0.0833 ≠ ₹83,300`).  
  - *C is incorrect* because ₹18,00,000 is the candidate's total annual salary, not the placement fee.  
  - *D is incorrect* because ₹3,60,000 reflects a 20% executive search retainer rate, not the agreed 8.33% rate.

### Question 4 (Order: 4)
**Topic / Category:** Contract Staffing Commercials (Billing Rates & Markups)  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 3 (Commercial Fee Structures: Direct Placement Fees vs Agency Markups) & File `3.3. Key Aspects in Contract Hiring from Staffing Agencies in India.pdf`  

**Question Statement:**  
In IT contract staffing, a staffing agency agrees on an hourly billing rate of ₹1,200 per hour with a client for a Cloud Architect. The agency pays the consultant a gross pay rate of ₹900 per hour. What term describes the ₹300 difference, and what costs does it cover?

- **A.** Placement discount; it is refunded to the client at year-end.
- **B.** Agency gross margin (markup); it covers statutory compliance, recruiter overheads, bench risk, insurance, and agency operational profit.
- **C.** Liquidated damages; it covers potential project delays.
- **D.** Statutory PF fine; it is remitted to the government as a penalty.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why B is correct:** The spread between client billing rate and candidate pay rate is the agency gross margin (markup). In contract staffing, this margin covers employer statutory contributions (PF, ESI, gratuity accrual), payroll financing, candidate benefits, recruiter incentives, operational costs, and business profit.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* because margins are retained by the staffing agency as service revenue, not refunded.  
  - *C is incorrect* because liquidated damages are breach-of-contract penalties, not contractual staffing margins.  
  - *D is incorrect* because legitimate statutory PF contributions are regular payroll components, not penalties.

### Question 5 (Order: 5)
**Topic / Category:** Client Replacement Guarantee Clauses  
**Difficulty:** Medium  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 2 (Staffing Models in the Indian IT Market) & File `3.3. Full-Time Hiring and Contract Hiring in INDIA.docx`  

**Question Statement:**  
What is the standard purpose of a "90-Day Free Replacement Guarantee" clause in a Master Services Agreement (MSA) for permanent FTE hiring?

- **A.** The client can return hardware assets to the staffing agency within 90 days without penalty.
- **B.** If a placed candidate resigns or is terminated for performance within 90 calendar days of joining, the agency must provide a suitable replacement candidate at no additional recruitment service charge.
- **C.** The candidate is permitted to work at multiple client companies simultaneously for 90 days.
- **D.** The agency guarantees that the candidate will receive a 90% salary hike within three months.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why B is correct:** The 90-day replacement clause (or "clawback/credit note" provision) protects client investments. If a placed candidate leaves or fails probationary performance criteria within the guarantee window (typically 90 days in India), the staffing partner is contractually obligated to source, screen, and place an equivalent replacement without charging a second placement fee.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* because hardware assets are managed by IT infrastructure vendors, not recruitment agencies.  
  - *C is incorrect* because dual employment (moonlighting) is strictly prohibited under Indian IT employment agreements.  
  - *D is incorrect* because salary revisions are strictly determined by the hiring client's internal appraisal policies.

### Question 6 (Order: 6)
**Topic / Category:** Practical Scenario: Analyzing FTE vs. C2H Candidate Suitability  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 4 (Case Study: Analyzing FTE vs C2H Job Descriptions) & File `Sample Job Description - Contract vs. a Full-time position.docx`  

**Question Statement:**  
A tier-1 IT consulting client requires a Senior React Native Developer for a critical 6-month banking app project starting in 15 days, with high likelihood of extension on client payroll if the pilot succeeds. The recruiter identifies a top-rated candidate who currently holds a permanent role at an MNC with a strict 90-day notice period and insists exclusively on direct permanent payroll with immediate medical coverage for their family. How should the recruiter handle this match?

- **A.** Persuade the candidate to abscond from their MNC employer immediately and join the contract role without serving their notice period.
- **B.** Modify the client's contract requisition in the portal to full-time without client approval.
- **C.** Disqualify the candidate for this specific C2H opening due to both notice period incompatibility (90 days vs. 15-day SLA) and contract payroll reluctance, and instead pipeline the candidate for upcoming direct FTE client requisitions matching their 90-day availability.
- **D.** Offer the candidate double their expected salary on personal verbal commitment without checking client bill rates.

**Correct Answer:** **C**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why C is correct:** In IT staffing, alignment of notice period, employment type preference (FTE vs. C2H), and project start dates is critical. A candidate requiring permanent direct payroll with a 90-day notice cannot fulfill an immediate 15-day C2H requirement. A professional recruiter acknowledges this mismatch, avoids wasting client bandwidth, and preserves the relationship by pipelining the candidate for matching FTE roles.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* because advising candidates to abscond breaches professional ethics, results in negative background verification (failed BGV), and prevents release of relieving letters.  
  - *B is incorrect* because recruiters cannot unilaterally change client commercial contracts or employment types.  
  - *D is incorrect* because compensation commitments must strictly align with pre-approved client billing rates and financial guidelines.
