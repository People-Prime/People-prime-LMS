### Question 1 (Order: 1)
**Topic / Category:** Core Compensation Terminology: CTC vs. Gross vs. Net Take-Home  
**Difficulty:** Easy  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 1 (Deciphering CTC: Cost to Company vs Gross Salary vs Net Take-Home) & File `8. Employee CTC Break-up.docx`  

**Question Statement:**  
In Indian corporate payroll, what is the fundamental difference between "Cost to Company (CTC)" and an employee's "Net In-Hand (Take-Home) Salary"?

- **A.** CTC and Net Take-Home are always 100% identical in all Indian companies.
- **B.** CTC represents the total annual financial expenditure incurred by the employer on the employee (including employer PF, gratuity, insurance, and bonuses), whereas Net Take-Home is the actual cash credited to the employee's bank account after deducting employee statutory taxes (PF, PT, TDS).
- **C.** CTC is the money the employee must pay to the company each month to keep their job.
- **D.** Net Take-Home includes company office electricity and building rental bills.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why B is correct:** Cost to Company (CTC) is an aggregate financial accounting metric comprising direct earnings (Basic, HRA, Allowances), employer statutory contributions (Employer PF 12%, Gratuity 4.81%, ESI if applicable), company-provided group insurance, and retiral provisions. Net Take-Home is the remaining net cash credited monthly after all employee deductions (Employee PF 12%, Professional Tax, and Income Tax/TDS) are subtracted from Gross Salary.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* because CTC is never identical to Take-Home due to mandatory statutory deductions and non-cash benefits.  
  - *C is incorrect* because compensation is paid by employers to employees, not vice versa.  
  - *D is incorrect* because building operational overheads are general corporate expenses, not employee salary components.

### Question 2 (Order: 2)
**Topic / Category:** Statutory Deductions: Employee Provident Fund (EPF) Rules  
**Difficulty:** Medium  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 2 (Statutory Deductions & Employer Contributions: PF, ESI, Gratuity & PT) & File `CTC structure in detail to understand.docx`  

**Question Statement:**  
Under the standard Indian Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act (EPF), what is the statutory percentage deducted from the employee's Basic Salary, and what is the matching contribution made by the employer?

- **A.** 50% employee deduction and 0% employer contribution.
- **B.** 12% of Basic Salary deducted from employee earnings, matched by an equal 12% contribution by the employer (which splits into EPF and EPS).
- **C.** 2% flat deduction regardless of salary.
- **D.** 100% of the entire gross salary is placed in the PF account.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why B is correct:** Under EPF regulations, 12% of the employee's Basic Salary (+ dearness allowance if applicable) is deducted monthly as the employee's contribution. The employer matches this with an equal 12% contribution, of which 8.33% goes to the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS, capped at statutory wage limits) and 3.67% goes to the EPF account.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* because EPF is a legally mandated 12% co-contribution scheme, not a 50% unilateral deduction.  
  - *C is incorrect* because 2% is below the statutory EPF rate.  
  - *D is incorrect* because EPF is calculated strictly on Basic Salary, not total gross compensation.

### Question 3 (Order: 3)
**Topic / Category:** Gratuity Calculation & Accrual Rules  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 2 (Statutory Deductions: Gratuity) & File `CTC structure in detail to understand.docx`  

**Question Statement:**  
In Indian employment law under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, what is the statutory eligibility requirement for an employee to receive gratuity payout, and what annual percentage of Basic Salary is typically budgeted in the CTC?

- **A.** 6 months of service; 50% of CTC.
- **B.** Minimum 5 years of continuous service with the same employer (except in cases of death or disablement); typically budgeted as ~4.81% of Basic Salary (representing 15 days of wages per completed year of service).
- **C.** 1 day of service; 100% of annual bonus.
- **D.** Gratuity is only payable if the employee wins an international award.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why B is correct:** Under the Payment of Gratuity Act, employees become eligible for gratuity upon separation after completing at least 5 years of continuous service. Employers accrue gratuity in the annual CTC breakdown as `(15 / 26) × Basic Monthly Salary`, which mathematically calculates to approximately 4.81% of the annual Basic Salary.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* because 6 months does not satisfy the statutory 5-year vesting requirement.  
  - *C is incorrect* because 1 day of employment does not confer gratuity entitlements.  
  - *D is incorrect* because gratuity is a statutory retiral benefit, not an discretionary achievement prize.

### Question 4 (Order: 4)
**Topic / Category:** Allowances & Compensation Structure: House Rent Allowance (HRA)  
**Difficulty:** Medium  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 3 (Tax-Saving Allowances & Variable Bonus) & File `8. Employee CTC Break-up.docx`  

**Question Statement:**  
In standard Indian corporate salary structuring, what is the customary proportion of Basic Salary allocated to House Rent Allowance (HRA) for employees living in metro cities (e.g., Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Chennai)?

- **A.** 5% of Basic Salary.
- **B.** 40% to 50% of Basic Salary (aligning with Income Tax exemptions under Section 10(13A)).
- **C.** 95% of total CTC.
- **D.** ₹100 flat per month.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why B is correct:** HRA is typically structured as 50% of Basic Salary in designated metro cities (or 40% in non-metro locations). This structure optimizes tax benefits for employees claiming HRA exemptions under Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act based on rent paid.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* because 5% is disproportionately low and leaves excessive compensation taxable under special allowances.  
  - *C is incorrect* because Basic Salary must constitute a substantial portion of CTC (typically 40–50%), meaning HRA cannot occupy 95% of total CTC.  
  - *D is incorrect* because a ₹100 flat allowance is obsolete and offers no tax relief.

### Question 5 (Order: 5)
**Topic / Category:** Professional Tax (PT) Statutory Deduction  
**Difficulty:** Easy  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 2 (Statutory Deductions: PT) & File `CTC breakup.docx`  

**Question Statement:**  
What is Professional Tax (PT) in Indian payroll calculations, and what is the typical maximum statutory deduction per month in states like Karnataka, Maharashtra, or Telangana?

- **A.** A tax levied on candidates who hold PhD degrees, costing ₹10,000 per month.
- **B.** A state-level tax levied on salaried professions, capped by the Indian Constitution at a maximum of ₹2,500 per year (typically deducted as ₹200 per month, with ₹300 in one month in states like Karnataka).
- **C.** A tax paid by the employer directly to the United Nations.
- **D.** A fee charged by recruitment agencies for reviewing candidate resumes.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why B is correct:** Professional Tax is a state government direct tax on employment under Article 276 of the Indian Constitution, with an annual ceiling of ₹2,500. In major IT hubs like Karnataka and Maharashtra, the standard deduction is ₹200 per month (adjusted to ₹300 in February or March to total ₹2,500 annually).
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* because PT applies to general salaried employees regardless of academic degrees, and cannot exceed ₹2,500 per year.  
  - *C is incorrect* because PT is collected by state commercial tax departments, not international bodies.  
  - *D is incorrect* because recruitment agencies are strictly prohibited from charging candidates fees for job applications.

### Question 6 (Order: 6)
**Topic / Category:** Practical Scenario: Salary Calculation & Hike Negotiation  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Source Lesson:** Submodule 4 (Step-by-Step Practical Payroll Calculations: Worked Examples) & File `Customized breakdown for a specific CTC amount.docx`  

**Question Statement:**  
A candidate currently earns a Total Fixed CTC of ₹10,00,000 per annum. A client extends an offer with a 30% hike on Fixed CTC. The candidate also receives a ₹1,00,000 joining bonus payable with their first month's salary, subject to a 1-year clawback clause. What is the candidate's new Annual Fixed CTC, and how should an IT recruiter explain the joining bonus to the candidate?

- **A.** New Fixed CTC is ₹14,00,000; the joining bonus is permanent annual salary that repeats every year.
- **B.** New Fixed CTC is ₹13,00,000; the ₹1,00,000 joining bonus is a one-time upfront cash incentive that does not increase base CTC in Year 2 and must be repaid if the candidate leaves before completing 12 months.
- **C.** New Fixed CTC is ₹10,30,000; the bonus is an optional voucher for company cafeteria snacks.
- **D.** New Fixed CTC is ₹30,00,000; the joining bonus is tax-free under all global laws.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:**  
- **Why B is correct:**  
  - `New Fixed CTC = Current CTC (₹10,00,000) + 30% Hike (₹3,00,000) = ₹13,00,000`.  
  - Recruiters must transparently explain compensation terminology: a joining bonus is a one-time cash incentive paid at onboarding, not part of recurring annual fixed CTC. Furthermore, it carries a standard 12-month clawback condition (must be repaid if the employee resigns before 1 year of service).
- **Why other options are incorrect:**  
  - *A is incorrect* because adding the bonus into recurring annual fixed CTC misleads the candidate about their second-year salary.  
  - *C is incorrect* because a 30% hike on 10 LPA is ₹3 LPA, not ₹30,000.  
  - *D is incorrect* because calculations are mathematically wrong and joining bonuses are fully taxable as regular income.
